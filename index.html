What it is

The AI design exploration engine for specialized silicon.

not an EDA replacementnot an RTL generatornot an LLM wrapper

Why teams use it

The decisions that set a chip's performance are made first, with the least evidence.

Array size, dataflow, precision and memory get fixed early, often from spreadsheets and intuition. The compiler team learns months later what the hardware can actually do. Every change to either side means another round of synthesis and place-and-route.

The engine turns that into one measured loop, so hardware and software are decided together, on evidence, before the expensive commitments.

From months of iterationDecide in weeks

From brute-force EDA runsCompute where it counts

From one-off studiesLearning that compounds

Every run, including failures, becomes design experience. Your next accelerator starts from what your last one taught it.

How it works

One loop from workload to the designs worth taking to implementation.

You bring workloads and constraints. The engine proposes candidates, compiles your models onto each, measures them at increasing fidelity, and learns from every result, good or failed.

01 · DEFINE

Workloads and budget

Model layers or tensor shapes, plus your area, power, latency and process targets.

in: ONNX / MLIR shapes, PPA budget
02 · EXPLORE

Architecture × compiler

The planner proposes hardware and finds the best compiler mapping for each candidate.

over a space of 1M+ combinations
03 · PROVE

Measured, not guessed

Analytical estimates, RTL simulation, synthesis, then place-and-route on your flow, cheapest first.

every result checked, every failure kept
04 · DELIVER

Pareto front + compiler

The designs worth implementing, each with RTL parameters, compiler mapping and the evidence behind it.

out: designs, mappings, run history

Adaptive EDA budgeting

The engine decides which candidate deserves expensive EDA time.

Each tier costs orders of magnitude more than the one before it. Instead of a fixed filter, the planner chooses where to spend: a candidate moves to the next tier only when it is likely to improve your trade-off, and every result updates that judgement.

Design intelligence

It learns why a design choice moves power, performance and area.

A generic optimizer learns which settings scored well. The engine works across the layers of the stack, runs controlled experiments on its own generators, and keeps every result. That is what makes its next recommendation better than the last.

01 · WORKLOAD → HARDWARE

What your models actually need

model→operators→tensor shapes→memory behaviour→compute intensity→hardware needs

Sizing starts from the work, not from last year's chip.

02 · HARDWARE → COMPILER

A mapping for every candidate

architecture→primitives→memory hierarchy→parallelism→MLIR mapping

Each design is judged by how well your workloads really run on it, not by its peak numbers.

03 · COMPILER ↔ PHYSICAL

Hardware is only as good as its mappings

tiling→data movement→buffer need→SRAM size→area & power

A large scratchpad is worth its area only if the compiler can use it. Choosing hardware and mapping together avoids paying for silicon the software never touches.

04 · EDA EXPERIENCE

Every run becomes design knowledge

architecture+compiler+RTL+synthesis+P&R→PPA & failures
The kind of rule it learns:large array + aggressive clock → timing and routing risklarge SRAM, small tiles → area spent for nothingtoo little buffer → mappings that do not fit

The platform

Four parts that work as one.

Architecture explorer

Searches the hardware space with the compiler in the loop

Array shape, dataflow, precision, memory hierarchy, banking and implementation settings, each judged by how well your workloads map onto it.

  • systolic arrays
  • weight / output stationary
  • INT8 · INT16
  • SRAM macros
Compiler generator

An MLIR path for every candidate design

Tiling, loop order and memory allocation are chosen per architecture, lowered from standard MLIR to the accelerator's own command stream, and verified bit-exact on its RTL.

  • linalg
  • transform dialect
  • IREE
  • command ISA
Evaluation fabric

Four fidelity tiers with adaptive budgeting

From millisecond estimates to routed layouts, on-prem or in your cloud account, against open tools or your existing synthesis and place-and-route scripts.

  • analytical model
  • Verilator
  • Yosys · OpenROAD
  • your commercial flow
Design intelligence

Picks the next best experiment

Models where designs fail and why, from timing margins to routing, and chooses the run most likely to move your Pareto front. It improves with every design history it sees.

  • multi-fidelity
  • failure-aware
  • design history
