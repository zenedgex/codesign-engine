The problem

Stop choosing chip architectures by guesswork.

The decisions that shape an accelerator, its array, memories, dataflow, precision and how the compiler maps the model onto it, are made early, when the least evidence is available. Teams can afford to evaluate only a handful of candidates, and changing course after implementation starts costs months.

Atelier Studio lets you explore far more hardware configurations against your actual AI workloads, performance targets, area limits and power budget, before committing to expensive implementation cycles.

  • Explore more design alternatives
  • Find better workload-to-hardware mappings
  • Reduce manual design-space exploration
  • Evaluate trade-offs with measurable evidence

What makes it different

Explore the hardware and compiler as one design space.

Traditional workflows evaluate architecture, mapping and implementation in repeated engineering loops. Atelier Studio coordinates these decisions, so your workload requirements, the compiler mapping and implementation results all inform the search for candidate designs.

Today, Explore evaluates each candidate in the search range with its own compiler search and ranks them against your targets. You choose which design to take further; RTL simulation and implementation then check it.

  1. Workload and constraints Your model and its frame or token rate, power, area and latency targets
  2. Candidate architectures Engines, array, memories and dataflow, from a reference architecture or your own RTL blocks
  3. Compiler mapping For each candidate, the compiler builds that design's mapping space, searches it and compiles its best program
  4. Evaluation in tiers Fast estimate, then the SoC model; RTL simulation, synthesis and floorplan for the designs worth it
  5. Ranked designs The designs that meet your targets, ranked by what you care about most, each with its evidence
Conventional workflowWith Atelier Studio
Engineers hand-pick a few candidate architecturesExplore evaluates many candidate architectures against your workload and targets in one run
Compiler mapping is worked out after the hardware is fixedEach candidate is compared with its own best compiler mapping, so architecture and mapping are judged together
Expensive implementation runs are needed to narrow the choicesFast estimates, checked against RTL simulation, rank the candidates; simulation and implementation evaluate the ones you pick
Each round of results feeds the next manual iterationEvery candidate is compiled, timed and ranked in the same run; you choose from ranked designs with their evidence, then build and simulate
