02 The loop

One loop from workload to the designs worth taking to implementation.

You bring workloads and constraints. The engine proposes candidates, compiles your models onto each, measures them at increasing fidelity, and learns from every result, good or failed.

Measured, not guessed

Analytical estimates, RTL simulation, synthesis, then place-and-route on your flow, cheapest first. Every result is checked, every failure kept.

out: designs, mappings, run history

03 Design intelligence

It learns why a design choice moves power, performance and area.

A generic optimizer learns which settings scored well. The engine works across the layers of the stack, runs controlled experiments on its own generators, and keeps every result. Choose a layer to see what it reasons about.

What your models actually need

model→operators→tensor shapes→memory behaviour→compute intensity→hardware needs

Sizing starts from the work, not from last year's chip. Shapes and data reuse decide how large the array should be, how much on-chip memory pays for itself, and which dataflow keeps the multipliers busy.

A mapping for every candidate

architecture→primitives→memory hierarchy→parallelism→MLIR mapping

Each design is judged by how well your workloads really run on it, not by its peak numbers. The compiler path is generated per candidate and verified bit-exact on that candidate's RTL.

Hardware is only as good as its mappings

tiling→data movement→buffer need→SRAM size→area & power

A large scratchpad is worth its area only if the compiler can use it, and a mapping is only possible if the hardware has room for it. Choosing both together avoids paying for silicon the software never touches.

Every run becomes design knowledge

architecture+compiler+RTL+synthesis+P&R→PPA & failures
The kind of rule it learns:large array + aggressive clock → timing and routing risklarge SRAM, small tiles → area spent for nothingtoo little buffer → mappings that do not fit

Failures are not discarded. They teach the engine where not to spend the next expensive run, and the next project starts from that experience.

04 The platform

Four parts that work as one.

Built on standard tools your teams already know: MLIR for the compiler, open or commercial EDA for measurement.

Architecture explorer

Searches the hardware space with the compiler in the loop

Array shape, dataflow, precision, memory hierarchy, banking and implementation settings, each judged by how well your workloads map onto it.

  • systolic arrays
  • weight / output stationary
  • INT8 · INT16
  • SRAM macros
Compiler generator

An MLIR path for every candidate design

Tiling, loop order and memory allocation chosen per architecture, lowered from standard MLIR to the accelerator's own command stream, verified bit-exact on its RTL.

  • linalg
  • transform dialect
  • IREE
  • command ISA
Evaluation fabric

Four fidelity tiers with adaptive budgeting

From millisecond estimates to routed layouts, on-prem or in your cloud account, against open tools or your existing synthesis and place-and-route scripts.

  • analytical model
  • Verilator
  • Yosys · OpenROAD
  • your commercial flow
Design intelligence

Picks the next best experiment

Models where designs fail and why, from timing margins to routing, and chooses the run most likely to move your Pareto front. It improves with every design history it sees.

  • multi-fidelity
  • failure-aware
  • design history
